The question

TabFM already learns in context, so what do training and a GPU add?

TabFM predicts a row from labelled context rows in one forward pass, with no training step. Fine-tuning keeps that interface and trains it on your table. We ran the same code on an Apple M4 laptop and on an NVIDIA L4 in GKE.

Before: glukicov/ltm and ltm_serve

Zero-shot TabFM matched a trained CatBoost model with no training; ltm_serve then served it on GKE with an L4 pool that scales from zero.

The paper: arXiv 2506.08982

Full fine-tuning makes TabPFNv2 state of the art on academic benchmarks; on small datasets the gains are often statistically insignificant.

The recipe

The training step calls the unmodified model

src/ltm_ft/finetune.py
loss = episode_loss(model, episode)
loss.backward()  # type: ignore[no-untyped-call]
master.gradients_to_masters()
torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(master.masters, config.grad_clip)
optimizer.step()
scheduler.step()
optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True)
master.masters_to_params()

No custom forward: frozen parameters do not require gradients, so autograd records nothing until the first trainable module.

Master weights: gradients move to float32 copies, AdamW updates those, and the rounded values go back into the bf16 model.

What to train

1.62B of the 1.64B parameters sit in the in-context-learning transformer. Training the encoders needs a backward pass through all 24 blocks. M4 memory is what the MPS driver held; L4 is CUDA peak allocation.

The trap

Same checkpoint, same rows: 59.8%, 65.0% or 70.2%

Why it matters: "upcast the weights you train to float32" is the textbook move, and here it changes the model before the first gradient step. bfloat16 also differs by device, so zero-shot and fine-tuned must be compared on the same machine.

The fix

Keep the model in bf16 and the optimiser's copies in float32

src/ltm_ft/finetune.py · class Float32Master
def __init__(self, params: list[nn.Parameter]) -> None:
    self.params = params
    self.masters = [p.detach().float().clone() for p in params]

def gradients_to_masters(self) -> None:
    for p, m in zip(self.params, self.masters, strict=True):
        m.grad = None if p.grad is None else p.grad.float()
        p.grad = None

@torch.no_grad()
def masters_to_params(self) -> None:
    for p, m in zip(self.params, self.masters, strict=True):
        p.copy_(m)

Step 0 is the stock model: a test asserts the prepared model's predictions equal the untouched checkpoint's.

Nothing to convert: the fine-tuned result is a plain bf16 TabFM the stock TabFMClassifier uses directly.

A table TabFM is bad at

The checkerboard leaves zero-shot TabFM far below the ceiling

Ten uniform features. The label depends only on which square of a 6×6 board the first two fall in: P(yes) is sigmoid(+3) on white squares, sigmoid(−3) on black.

Why it is hard

Neither feature is informative alone, and a row's nearest neighbours in ten dimensions are mostly on other squares.

The gap, data seed 7

Test accuracy: zero-shot 69.1% on the M4, 66.5% on the L4; ceiling 94.9%.

The variant the laptop could not run

Training the encoders took the checkerboard from 66.5% to 93.8%

Test, data seed 7: zero-shot 66.5%, last 4 blocks 66.9%, + encoders 93.8% (log loss 0.623 → 0.224; ceiling 94.9%, 0.202).

Five seeds later

The encoder variant reaches the ceiling on 2 of 5 seeds

Seeds 7 and 37 reach 93.8% and 94.1%. Seeds 17, 27 and 47 stay at zero-shot; zero-shot itself ranges from 49.3% to 88.5%. 20 minutes on the L4.

Five seeds later

When it fails, training collapses to predicting 0.5

Seed 17 collapses for 150 steps, then recovers to 94.2% validation accuracy by step 300. Seeds 27 and 47 never leave ln 2 at lr 1e-4, 3e-4 or 1e-3.

Honest limits

Read these effects as a floor, and the big one as fragile

Scale
1,500 training rows, at most 150 steps (300 for diagnostics), 17–18% of the model. The paper trains the whole model on an 80 GB GPU on larger datasets.
Seeds
Five seeds on one synthetic task, chosen because zero-shot TabFM is weak on it; one seed for the others.
Hardware
One L4 is the largest GPU the project's quota allows. Laptop timings vary with other load: the same test pass took 52 s and 126 s.
Licence
TabFM weights are non-commercial: none are committed or baked into images. Code is MIT. non-commercial weights
Closing

Takeaways and try it

What we found

  • A dedicated GPU pays off the moment you iterate: speed made the seed check affordable
  • A laptop runs out of memory before it runs out of speed
  • Run more than one seed before you believe a fine-tuning win
  • Precision and device are part of the model; check the headroom first
